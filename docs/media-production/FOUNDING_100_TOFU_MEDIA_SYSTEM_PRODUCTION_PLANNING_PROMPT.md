# 1. Product Context

StrategicAI is building Operational Intelligence from Operational Reality.

The core customer-facing idea is:

# Operational Intelligence built from how your business actually works.

Plain-English translation, not primary positioning:

> **An AI that knows your business.**

Most AI tools begin with almost no understanding of the company using them.

The user must repeatedly explain:

* who people are;
* what they do;
* how work moves;
* what systems are used;
* where problems happen;
* what depends on what;
* what matters;
* who has authority;
* what changed;
* what is known;
* what is uncertain.

StrategicAI reverses that model.

StrategicAI first forms an evidence-bounded understanding of the business
itself.

This understanding can include:

* people;
* responsibilities;
* workflows;
* systems;
* customers;
* goals;
* dependencies;
* handoffs;
* bottlenecks;
* decisions;
* sources of information;
* recurring failures;
* owner dependencies;
* disagreements;
* unknowns;
* things that have not yet been made explicit.

StrategicAI builds this understanding.

The AI agent the customer interacts with is called:

# Nemo

The simple distinction is:

> **StrategicAI builds the understanding. Nemo is how the owner works with that understanding.**

For this campaign, the stronger simple distinction is:

> **StrategicAI builds the understanding. Nemo works from it with you.**

Do not use the internal name “SAR” in customer-facing materials.

# 3. The 90-Day Experience

The 90 days must never be framed as:

> “Ask AI questions for 90 days.”

That makes the product sound like better ChatGPT.

The actual behavior is:

# Interrogate the operating business.

The owner works against a living model of the company.

They can:

* inspect it;
* challenge it;
* correct it;
* follow dependencies;
* investigate bottlenecks;
* test assumptions;
* expose missing information;
* compare perspectives;
* trace causes;
* identify owner dependence;
* identify broken handoffs;
* examine hiring pressure;
* identify automation opportunities;
* understand downstream effects of changes.

A useful metaphor is:

# Use Nemo like a flashlight inside your company.

Point the flashlight at something that bothers you and follow the trail.

Examples:

* “Show me everything that still depends on me.”
* “If Amy is gone for two weeks, what stops moving?”
* “Where can a customer get stuck between sale and delivery?”
* “Who needs to know when inventory changes?”
* “Where do we enter the same information twice?”
* “Which problems do we keep fixing instead of solving?”
* “What are people watching that software could watch?”
* “What happens across the company when a product is late?”
* “What would break first if we grew 30% next month?”
* “Where do two people see the same process differently?”
* “What am I not seeing?”

The simple 90-day progression is:

## Month 1 — SEE IT

Build the first real picture.

Find gaps.

Correct what StrategicAI gets wrong.

Surface knowledge that had been living in people's heads.

## Month 2 — CHALLENGE IT

Pick problems and pull on them.

Ask:

* Why does this happen?
* Who depends on this?
* Where does it break?
* Who should know?
* Who owns it?
* What are we missing?

## Month 3 — CHANGE IT

Decide what deserves action.

Ask:

* What should we fix?
* What should we stop?
* What should we automate?
* What needs clearer ownership?
* What system needs to tell another system something changed?
* What deserves deeper work?

Campaign shorthand:

# See it. Challenge it. Change it.

The payoff of that progression is not merely a set of maps. The owner should imagine bringing Nemo the questions they would normally save for an operator, strategist, consultant, or trusted advisor:

* “What is still too dependent on me?”
* “Do we really need another person, and what problem would that solve?”
* “Where does this process really break?”
* “What should we fix before we automate?”
* “What would break first if we grew 30%?”
* “Of these three problems, which deserves attention first?”

The intended feeling is: **I already know what I would ask Nemo.**

# 7. TOFU Creative Principle

The TOFU layer is built around:

# Pain moments, not demographic personas.

This remains the governing principle for the `PS` track. A separate `CC`
Category Contrast track may begin with a familiar market moment—an agent offer,
an automation pitch, or an automation-first question—then challenge the
starting assumption and introduce Operational Reality.

```text
PS reaction = “That happened in my business.”
CC reaction = “Maybe automation is not the first question.”
```

Do not merge the tracks analytically or turn Category Contrast into generic AI
commentary.

Do not create avatars such as:

> “Mike, age 52, owns an HVAC business, likes golf.”

That is not useful here.

Instead create and target **recognition states**.

The same owner may move through several of these states during one week.

The goal of the first 2–3 seconds is:

# “That is me.”

Each clip should therefore focus on **one painful moment only**.

Do not combine:

* owner bottlenecks;
* broken handoffs;
* automation;
* visibility;
* hiring;
* software fragmentation;

all in one clip.

One clip = one pain moment.

The webinar connects the larger thesis.

# 8. Initial Pain-State Avatar Set

There are 18 initial recognition avatars.

## Tier 1 — Highest Priority

### 1. The Human Router

Moment:

Everyone comes to the owner to decide what should happen next.

Internal thought:

> “Why am I the switchboard for this company?”

Core territory:

* every decision comes to the owner;
* everyone asks “what next?”;
* work waits when owner is unavailable;
* owner has accidentally become part of the workflow.

---

### 2. The Company-in-My-Head Owner

Moment:

The business works because the owner remembers history, exceptions, promises, odd rules, and context.

Internal thought:

> “If I disappeared, they would be screwed.”

Core territory:

* bus test;
* weird unwritten rules;
* owner repeatedly explains the company;
* company memory vs owner memory.

---

### 3. The Firefighter

Moment:

The entire day disappears into exceptions, approvals, interruptions, and “quick questions.”

Internal thought:

> “I did none of the work I planned today.”

Core territory:

* planned day destroyed;
* “quick question” interruptions;
* repeated fires;
* owner's usefulness becomes the trap.

---

### 4. The Broken-Handoff Owner

Moment:

One part of the company believes work was handed off while the next part says it never received what it needed.

Internal thought:

> “Why does everything fall apart between people?”

Core territory:

* sales → ops;
* “I thought they had it”;
* missing information;
* customer feels internal handoff failures.

---

### 5. The Late-Problem Owner

Moment:

The owner learns about problems only after damage has already happened.

Internal thought:

> “Why am I always finding out last?”

Core territory:

* owner finds out too late;
* problems get more expensive over time;
* software knew before owner did;
* “Why didn't anyone tell me?”

---

### 6. The “We Need Another Person” Owner

Moment:

Someone is overloaded and the first instinct is to hire.

Internal thought:

> “I think we need another employee... but I am not sure.”

Core territory:

* hire before diagnosing;
* busy does not always equal understaffed;
* work bouncing around;
* hire versus fix the system.

---

### 7. The Tool-Stacked but Still Blind Owner

Moment:

The business has many systems but the owner still has to ask people what is happening.

Internal thought:

> “We have all this software. Why can't I see the business?”

Core territory:

* six tools, three phone calls;
* dashboards without answers;
* information exists but is disconnected;
* software islands.

---

### 8. The Can't-Take-a-Vacation Owner

Moment:

The owner leaves and decisions wait, work slows, or the phone follows them.

Internal thought:

> “Why can't I actually leave?”

Core territory:

* vacation test;
* owner phone follows them;
* decisions wait;
* absence reveals hidden dependencies.

# 21. Constraints

* StrategicAI
* Nemo

* SAR

> **StrategicAI builds the understanding. Nemo works from it with you.**

Use “business-aware advisor” only as a bounded value layer: Nemo works from the company context built and corrected with the owner. Do not collapse StrategicAI and Nemo into one magical or autonomous authority.

Keep customer-facing copy extremely simple.

Aim below what feels intellectually necessary.

Avoid terms such as:

* ontology;
* semantic model;
* cognition;
* substrate;
* orchestration;
* authority ledger;
* evidence architecture;
* persistent context;
* agentic infrastructure.

Instead of:

* “unlock the power of AI”;
* “AI transformation”;
* “revolutionize your business”;
* “future-proof”;
* “10x productivity”;
* “cutting-edge AI”;
* generic digital-transformation language.

The power comes from specificity.

## Do not overpromise

* knows everything immediately;
* automatically fixes the business;
* replaces management;
* guarantees revenue;
* eliminates employees;
* has integrations that are not yet available;

evidence and authority, or erase automation-builder partner optionality.
