# Publishing, Experiment, Measurement, and Feedback System

## I. Publishing plan

### Release principle

This system operates under the Market Learning Charter. Pain, category terms,
contrast intensity, and intended-experience language are controlled hypotheses.
Every post instance must resolve to an experiment ID, truth class, exact asset,
attribution path, and eventual `PROMOTE | RETEST | DEMOTE | RETIRE` decision.

Market response does not prove runtime capability or commercial readiness.

Publish natively, preserve the same clip ID, adapt the surrounding copy and CTA path, and stagger platforms enough that the founder can respond. The spoken pain or category premise and its reframe remain stable for a clip; platform behavior changes around them.

The governing acquisition path is defined in
`14_COMMENT_SEE_FUNNEL_AND_MESSAGE_CARD_CONTRACT.md`:

```text
PS / CC / MC -> COMMENT SEE -> DM -> 5 MIN or 35 MIN
                                      -> OFFER -> CHECKOUT only when commercially authorized
```

`SEE` is the first measurable conversion. No email or identity gate may precede
content. Direct-link/webinar-registration instructions below are superseded
where encountered; they must not be copied into a live post or automation.

Wave 1 publishes all eight clips to Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels. LinkedIn receives all eight over three weeks. TikTok begins with four representative clips and earns the other four through qualified signal. YouTube Shorts receives all eight as a slower searchable/discovery archive.

The matched C1–C4 Direct Attack Test publishes as a separate `CC` track after
its B02 claim review. C1 and C4 enter market during Week 2; C2 and C3 follow in
early Week 3. The softer/general CC01–CC04 slate remains backlog. None of these
assets enters the Wave 1 pain-state leaderboard or changes the requirement to
publish all eight pain-state clips.

### Repost and reuse policy

- Story reshares are allowed on the original publish day and once before the webinar event.
- Do not repost the identical Reel/Short to the same account within 21 days.
- A winner may return after 21–30 days only with a new hook, meaningful edit variation, or updated founder context.
- Do not upload a file containing another platform’s watermark.
- Reuse clips immediately in one-to-one outbound when the pain matches the prospect.
- A paid version retains the organic clip ID and adds a new post instance/edit ID.
- If paid promotion begins, prefer boosting a qualified organic post when that preserves useful comments and identity; do not turn raw views into the decision metric.

### Advisor-value publishing rule

The public post still earns recognition first. The advisor layer belongs after the pain moment and reframe, in a caption, spoken bridge, comment response, webinar invitation, or follow-up—not as an opening product claim. Use the question:

> **What would you ask if your advisor already knew your business?**

only when the clip has earned the owner’s attention. The destination remains the
one `Comment SEE` CTA. The DM carries the two content choices; do not add a
second public CTA for Nemo, an application, or a purchase.

### Category Contrast publishing rule

Category Contrast may begin with an AI-agent or automation market moment, but
must turn quickly toward the positive territory: how the business actually
works. The clip must make clear that automation remains a possible outcome.

Use a single category claim, a single plain-language question, and the `SEE`
CTA. AI automation agencies may be named and criticized as a category. Do not
tag a specific agency, identify an individual creator, stitch competitor ads,
fabricate misconduct, or use anti-worker/replacement framing to increase reach.

Publish `CC` clips far enough apart from `PS` clips to preserve response
coverage and clean attribution. Compare Category Contrast clips with other
Category Contrast clips, not directly with a pain-state recognition score.

### Message Card publishing rule

`MC` is a controlled language test, not a low-effort substitute for video.
Every published card requires a row in `MESSAGE_CARD_MANIFEST_TEMPLATE.csv`
and an ID matching `F100-MC-{TERRITORY}-{NN}`. Hold background, typography,
layout, brand treatment, CTA, destination choices, audience, and distribution
policy constant where practical; change the hook. Use the same `SEE` transport
gate as `PS` and `CC`, and keep `MC` results in their own comparison set.

### Direct Attack Test publishing rule

The `DIRECT_ATTACK_TEST` is a matched-belief experiment across `C1` through
`C4`. Hold the thesis, body structure, CTA, target audience, distribution
window, and paid-support policy as constant as practical. Declare
`conflict_intensity` on every post-instance record.

C3 and C4 require explicit G-04 conflict review before publication. Review for
truth, defensibility, owner relevance, unnecessary factual allegation, and
brand risk. Do not require neutral wording merely because the hook is
provocative.

Success is movement through the qualified-owner funnel:

```text
attention
    -> qualified owner recognition
    -> SEE comment
    -> DM delivered
    -> 5-minute or 35-minute content
    -> qualified conversation or other truthful next step

OPTIONAL, ONLY AFTER COMMERCIAL AUTHORITY
    -> offer
    -> verified checkout
    -> paid tenant
```

Views, arguments, stitches, and angry automation-provider comments do not make
an aggressive clip a winner. A smaller C3/C4 asset that produces qualified
owner content and commercial progression can defeat a high-reach outrage asset.

### Comment and founder response behavior

Response coverage is part of production, not an afterthought.

- Reserve 20 minutes after posting and two 15-minute response blocks later that day.
- Answer qualified questions within one business day.
- Acknowledge the specific business moment before mentioning StrategicAI.
- Ask one clarifying question that helps the owner describe a real incident.
- Never diagnose the commenter’s company or debate whether the owner is “doing it wrong.”
- Classify the responder as `OWNER`, `OPERATOR`, `EXECUTIVE`, `AUTOMATION_PROVIDER`, `AI_BUILDER`, `LOW_FIT`, or `UNKNOWN` when the evidence supports it.
- Record automation-provider defense separately from owner objection; do not pile on, quote-dunk, or treat competitor anger as validation.
- Move private or sensitive details to DM, then log only the permitted summary.
- Do not paste a content link publicly under `SEE`. The authorized transport sends the two tracked choices privately without an identity gate.
- Record strong phrases verbatim in the market signal log, with source and consent boundaries.

Approved response patterns, pending release review:

| Signal | Founder response pattern |
| --- | --- |
| “This is exactly my company.” | “What was the moment this week that made it feel exact?” |
| “Isn’t this just ChatGPT?” | “ChatGPT starts from what you tell it in the conversation. StrategicAI’s job is to build a business picture you can inspect and correct. The webinar shows the difference.” |
| “My business is too messy.” | “Messy is useful information. The first picture does not have to be perfect; it has to show what is known and what still needs asking.” |
| “Can it connect to QuickBooks?” | “That depends on the approved delivery scope, so I will not promise an integration in a comment. The first question is what information matters and how it is used.” |
| “Can it help my sales team?” | “Sales can be one area to inspect. The starting point is understanding how leads, decisions, and handoffs work in your business.” |
| “Good automation agencies already do discovery.” | “Good. Then we agree on the important part: understand the business before building against it.” Log `CONFLICT-PARTNER` when the provider describes a compatible practice. |
| “This is just anti-automation rage bait.” | “Automation can be the right answer. My argument is that it should follow an understanding of the business it is changing.” Log the responder segment and whether this is qualified brand risk or provider defense. |

## 35-minute walkthrough narrative requirement

The media system now depends on a 35-minute walkthrough that can receive both pain-state and
Category Contrast traffic. For the Category Contrast entry, record this belief
sequence as a requirement rather than rewriting the canonical webinar here:

```text
“I need AI automation.”
    -> “I need to understand what should actually be automated.”
    -> “I want to understand how my business actually works.”
    -> “Is Operational Reality the right name for that?”
    -> “I want to know what StrategicAI would find.”
    -> “I already know what I would ask Nemo.”
```

A simple opening territory to test is:

> **Your feed is full of people offering AI agents to automate parts of your business. But almost everyone is starting in the middle. Before AI acts inside a business, somebody—or something—needs to understand how that business actually works.**

“Almost everyone” is test copy and requires claim review. The webinar should
state the positive sequence plainly:

```text
Operational Reality
    -> understanding
    -> Operational Intelligence
    -> decision
    -> automation, agents, process change, alerts,
       ownership, human judgment, or no change when warranted
```

The media system also depends on a webinar that earns more than “AI should know the business first.” The webinar must earn:

> **Once it knows the business, I have an advisor worth bringing serious questions to.**

The webinar brief should include a moment like:

> When was the last time you had an advisor who already understood the people, systems, bottlenecks, history, and strange little rules inside your company before you asked the question?

Then ask:

> **What would you ask if you did?**

The existing close remains important:

> **If Nemo knew your business tomorrow, what would you ask first?**

The presentation must explain why the answer matters while preserving the boundary that Nemo is not omniscient, professionally licensed, automatically authoritative, or a replacement for human judgment. This is a required dependency of G-02 and G-08. Do not rewrite a canonical webinar file in this task; record the revision requirement and validate the final webinar before media release.

## Content, offer, and post-payment narrative dependency

The short/deep content may test the intended value proposition below. Offer,
onboarding, and post-payment use requires separate truth-layer and Commercial
Commitment review:

- StrategicAI is being built to form evidence-bounded company context.
- Business Views are intended to let the owner inspect and improve bounded
  parts of that context.
- Nemo is intended to reason from the context lawfully available in the named
  experience.
- A 90-day serious-question experience is proposed commercial architecture,
  not current delivery fact.
- Durable context may create compounding value when the named runtime verifies
  its availability and retention boundary.

Known dependencies remain unresolved and must not be implied as complete: production Formation destination, Stripe Checkout Session/payment verification, Terms route, Privacy route, and post-payment handoff copy. Frontend and payment implementation remain outside this task.

## J. Experiment design

### Wave 1 question

Wave 1 asks:

> Which pain states make qualified owners stop, recognize themselves, comment
> `SEE`, consume content, and take the next truthful step?

Offer progression becomes a valid experimental outcome only after the
commercial branch is authorized.

It does not cleanly isolate hook type because each pain state begins with a different creative mechanism. Treat Wave 1 as pain-state screening. Hook isolation begins with the second angle inside a winning or ambiguous pain state.

### Category Contrast question

The separate `CC` track asks:

> **Does challenging automation-first thinking create qualified `SEE`, content, and offer intent?**

Secondary questions:

- Does “Operational Reality” resonate, or must “how the business actually works” lead?
- Does StrategicAI feel more considered than an automation-first offer without sounding anti-automation?
- Does the viewer still understand that automation and agents may be valid downstream outcomes?
- Does the contrast generate serious owner questions?
- Does it attract automation consultants or agencies as possible partners?
- Does it create the false expectation that StrategicAI implements every identified change?

Track the last question as expectation risk. Do not combine `CC` results with
pain-state screening totals. Compare hold, completion, qualified `SEE`, content
progression, offer behavior, and coded signals within Category Contrast first.

### Conflict-intensity question

The matched Direct Attack Test asks:

> **Does higher conflict intensity create more qualified-owner `SEE`, content,
> and truthful next-step behavior than lower-intensity expression of the same
> understanding-first belief?**

Compare C1–C4 on owner-segment hold, qualified `SEE` comments, DM delivery,
content starts/completions, and qualified conversations. Add offer views,
checkout clicks, and paid tenants only after the commercial branch activates.
Read these alongside
`CONFLICT-OWNER-RES`, `CONFLICT-AGENCY-DEF`, `CONFLICT-PARTNER`,
`CONFLICT-BRAND-RISK`, and `CONFLICT-CURIOSITY`. Competitor reaction is a
distribution effect, not evidence of owner demand.

### Market-language naming test

```text
MARKET_LANGUAGE_TEST = HOW_THE_BUSINESS_ACTUALLY_WORKS_VS_OPERATIONAL_REALITY
DEFAULT_ENTRY_LANGUAGE = HOW_THE_BUSINESS_ACTUALLY_WORKS
NAMED_TERRITORY = OPERATIONAL_REALITY
DECISION_STATE = OPEN
```

Do not assume owners will spontaneously say “Operational Reality.” Track
comprehension, repetition, qualified `SEE`, content/offer progression, and exact language
for both expressions. Plain language leads until evidence shows the named
territory earns comprehension quickly enough to lead.

### What remains constant

- Founder and core delivery posture.
- 9:16 capture and restrained edit template.
- 25–45 second duration boundary.
- One pain moment, one reframe, one `SEE` CTA.
- Approved short/deep content roles and offer promise.
- DM copy, content assets, routes, offer transition, and transport policy during each comparison window.
- Clip-level UTM structure and dashboard definitions.
- Platform-specific posting windows within a consistent range.
- No paid boost during the first 48 hours unless the entire wave uses the same policy.

### What changes in Wave 1

- Pain avatar and family.
- Hook mechanism.
- concrete business moment;
- environment and emotional delivery appropriate to the moment;
- approved CTA wording, recorded as a test dimension.

Because several dimensions change together, the first read identifies promising packages. It does not prove which single dimension caused performance.

### Signal ladder

| Level | Evidence | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S0 Distribution | Impressions/reach sufficient to observe behavior | If absent, the clip is under-distributed; do not call it a pain failure |
| S1 Attention | 3-second hold, average watch time, retention curve, completion | Hook and delivery earned or lost attention |
| S2 Recognition | Saves, shares, qualified comments, DMs, “that is us” language | Pain state feels real to the right audience |
| S3 Intent | Qualified `SEE`, DM delivery, short/deep selection, content start/completion, offer visit | The asset created enough curiosity for the next step |
| S3Q Question intent | A prospect voluntarily names a serious business question they would bring to Nemo | The owner moved from “interesting” to “I already know what I would ask” |
| S4 Commercial | Application, qualified conversation, paid tenant, assisted conversion | The pain state contributes to actual acquisition |

### Advisor resonance and question intent

Add a qualitative layer to the performance read. Do not turn small-sample question behavior into fake statistical precision.

**Advisor resonance** is present when an owner says or implies:

- “I would use this every day.”
- “I already know what I would ask it.”
- “This would be like having another operator.”
- “Could I ask it about hiring?”
- “Could it help me decide between X and Y?”

**Serious-question intent** (`INT-QUESTION`) is present when a prospect voluntarily names a real business question or decision they would bring to Nemo. Examples include hiring, bottleneck, pricing, capacity, automation, customer issue, role decision, system choice, or growth constraint.

Record:

- whether a clip generated a real owner question;
- the question category;
- the consequence level (`CURIOUS`, `OPERATING`, `MATERIAL_DECISION`);
- whether the person registered, attended, applied, or paid;
- whether the question creates expectation risk or a product discovery item.

Repeated consequential question intent is high-value qualitative evidence. It should feed scripts, short/deep content, the offer, Formation, the 90-day experience, and product capability discovery.

### Consultant-comparison signal

Capture both resonance and risk:

- positive: “This would save me consulting time,” “A consultant would need to learn all this first.”
- comparison question: “How is this different from a consultant?”
- expectation risk: “Would Nemo replace my consultant?”

The defensible comparison is accumulated, inspectable, correctable company context—not better advice, superior accuracy, or universal consultant replacement.

### Minimum useful signal

Use sufficiency rules, not fake statistical significance:

- Wait seven full days after each post instance before a final Wave 1 decision.
- Require at least two native platform placements or one placement with enough distribution to produce a visible retention curve.
- As an operating flag—not a scientific threshold—mark a clip `UNDER_DISTRIBUTED` when it has fewer than roughly 300 impressions or 100 three-second viewers on a platform. Repost/repackage before judging pain.
- Do not make a commercial winner claim from one `SEE` or content start. Treat it as a case to inspect.
- Repeated qualified language across comments, DMs, outbound, content feedback, and sales calls can outweigh a small numeric difference in views.
- A paid tenant or strongly qualified application is a higher-value signal than thousands of low-intent views.

### Decision states

| State | Use when | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `EXPAND` | Strong recognition plus at least one credible downstream intent/commercial signal | Produce two materially different angles for the avatar |
| `ITERATE_HOOK` | Weak 3-second hold but strong recognition among those who stay, or matching pain appears elsewhere | Keep pain; test a different hook type and first-frame delivery |
| `ITERATE_BODY` | Strong hold, sharp drop before reframe/CTA | Shorten examples, clarify turn, or move reframe earlier |
| `ITERATE_CTA` | Strong completion/recognition but weak profile/click behavior | Test one CTA and ensure link path is obvious |
| `HOLD` | Interesting qualitative signal but insufficient distribution or downstream volume | Preserve; revisit after more traffic or outreach use |
| `KILL_FOR_NOW` | Two distinct hooks with adequate distribution produce weak recognition and no meaningful downstream signal | Stop production; record revisit trigger |

### When to produce more variations

Produce the next two angles when:

- the clip creates qualified comments/DMs and measurable content or offer behavior;
- the same pain improves outbound reply quality;
- one platform shows strong owner-quality signal that can be retested elsewhere;
- the pain is commercially active even if view volume is modest.

Do not expand solely because the clip has the most views, likes, or broad emotional comments.

### When to kill or revisit

Kill for now only after the pain has received two genuinely different hooks, adequate distribution, and a stable `SEE`/content-funnel period. Revisit when:

- market conditions or offer framing changes;
- outbound or customer discovery repeatedly names the pain;
- a new concrete moment provides better language;
- content response proves the pain matters but the clip did not express it;
- a different channel contains a more plausible owner audience.

### Failure diagnosis map

| Observed pattern | Most likely issue | Test next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low 3-second hold | Hook wording, first frame, delivery, or wrong audience distribution | New hook mechanism; tighter first frame; inspect traffic source |
| Good hold, early mid-clip drop | Script too broad, too slow, or second pain entered | Cut examples; move reframe earlier; one moment only |
| Good completion, few qualified `SEE` comments | Weak product bridge, unclear CTA, or low-intent entertainment | New bridge/CTA; inspect commenter quality before blaming pain |
| `SEE` comments, low DM delivery | Transport, permission, keyword, or platform failure | Transport QA before changing media |
| DM delivered, no content selection | Choice copy, link rendering, or expectation mismatch | Inspect delivery receipt and test choice labels |
| Content route loaded, no video start | Asset/player/load failure or expectation mismatch | Player and mobile QA before changing media |
| Content complete, low offer visits | Belief change, offer bridge, CTA clarity, or qualification | Review retention and revise content-to-offer transition |
| Offer views, low checkout clicks | Offer economics, scope, trust, or payment clarity | Interview non-buyers; inspect objections and terms |
| Checkout clicks, low verified purchase | Payment friction or verification gap | Do not diagnose until server-side verification exists |
| Paid conversions, delivery strain | Capacity rather than media | Pause scale according to G-07 thresholds |

### Hook-failure versus pain-failure test

1. If hold is weak, keep the pain and test a new hook before demoting it.
2. If hold is healthy but recognition language is absent, the pain expression may be generic or the audience wrong.
3. If the same pain appears in webinar/outbound language, the clip failed even if the pain did not.
4. If two different hooks earn attention but neither produces recognition or intent, mark the pain `KILL_FOR_NOW`.

### Media-failure versus funnel-surface-failure test

- Compare observed `SEE` comments with DM-delivery receipts. A gap suggests transport failure.
- Compare DM choice clicks with `see_short`/`see_deep` route arrivals. A gap suggests link or analytics failure.
- Compare video starts/completions and offer visits by originating asset.
- If multiple strong assets lose progression at the same route or device, fix the surface.
- If one asset uniquely produces low-quality `SEE` or offer behavior, its hook may be curiosity-heavy and qualification-light.
- Keep transport, content, and offer changes timestamped; do not compare assets across an unrecorded funnel change.

### Standard formulas

Use the same denominator throughout the campaign and record when a platform’s definition differs:

```text
3-second hold rate = three_second_views / plays_or_eligible_impressions
average watch percentage = average_watch_time_sec / actual_length_sec
completion rate = completions / plays
profile action rate = profile_actions / plays
qualified SEE rate = qualified_see_comments / eligible_reach
DM delivery rate = see_dm_delivered / see_comments
content selection rate = (see_short + see_deep) / see_dm_delivered
content start rate = video_starts / selected_content_route_arrivals
content completion rate = video_completes / video_starts
content-to-offer rate = content_to_offer / unique_content_sessions
checkout-click rate = checkout_clicks / offer_views
paid conversion = paid_tenants / qualified_applications
cost per qualified SEE = spend / qualified_see_comments
cost per paid tenant = spend / paid_tenants
```

Never compare platform-native “views” without checking each platform’s definition. Use within-platform trends first and full-funnel business outcomes across platforms.

### Attribution model

Every observable funnel event retains the available governed object:

```text
message_card_id, originating_clip_id, track, hook_family, exact_hook,
conflict_intensity, source, platform, post_instance_id,
utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content
```

Public URLs use only the compact allowlist in the governing `SEE` contract;
`exact_hook` resolves through the asset manifest and is not placed in the URL.
Opportunity-level first/last/assisted attribution may be joined later only
after lawful identity capture. It is not a reason to gate content or put
personal data in query parameters.

### Daily review

Timebox to 15 minutes:

- verify posts, `SEE` transport, captions, analytics, and attribution ingestion;
- respond and log comments/DMs;
- flag technical failures or accidental claims immediately;
- note unusual retention or qualified behavior;
- make no pain-state kill decision.

### Weekly review

Timebox to 60 minutes with founder, marketing owner, and funnel owner:

1. Read S0–S4 in order; do not begin with views.
2. Compare pain states within platform and across outbound-assisted use.
3. Compare Category Contrast only within the `CC` track; do not merge it into pain-state ranking.
4. Compare matched C1–C4 Direct Attack assets by qualified-owner funnel outcome, not raw engagement.
5. Read exact comments, objections, content responses, SEE reasons, and non-buyer language.
6. Separate owner/operator/executive behavior from automation-provider, AI-builder, low-fit, and unknown reaction.
7. Read advisor-resonance, serious-question, understanding-first, plain “how the business actually works,” named “Operational Reality,” partner, conflict, consultant-comparison, and expectation-risk signals separately.
8. Audit funnel changes and tracking gaps.
9. Assign one decision state per clip.
10. Select no more than four next assets.
11. Confirm delivery capacity before increasing promotion.

### Capture rules

- Log source URL, date, platform, clip ID, signal code, exact phrase, and owner role if known.
- For direct-attack assets, also log experiment ID, conflict intensity, and audience segment.
- Do not classify automation-provider defense as qualified-owner resonance.
- Do not infer company facts beyond what the person states.
- Mark consent before using a name, logo, quote, or story publicly.
- Separate market language from product evidence. A request for an integration is demand signal, not proof that the integration exists.
- Link each signal to the decision it changed.
