# Campaign and Audience Architecture

## A. Campaign architecture

### Campaign objective

Create enough specific recognition and trust that qualified local SMB owners
who encounter StrategicAI through outreach or media comment `SEE`, choose the
5-minute or 35-minute ungated content path, and reveal which operating problems,
category expressions, and intended-experience ideas earn a truthful next step.

The active media scoreboard is qualified `SEE`, content selection, consumption,
language evidence, and qualified conversation. Paid-tenant progression becomes
a valid commercial scoreboard only after the offer and delivery branch is
authorized.

### The TOFU job

Each pain-state clip must do four things and stop:

1. Name one painful operating moment the owner experienced recently.
2. Reframe that moment as a problem in how the business is understood, connected, or carried—not merely a bad employee or lack of software.
3. Introduce the possibility of building a useful, correctable picture of the business and, where natural, Nemo working from that context.
4. Earn the comment `SEE`.

The clip does not teach the product, diagnose the viewer, explain architecture, or solve the business problem.

Category Contrast clips have a parallel job: enter the existing AI-agent and
automation conversation, challenge automation as the automatic starting point,
introduce Operational Reality, and earn the same `SEE` comment. They are
measured separately from pain-state screening.

### Relationship to the content funnel

The asset creates recognition or challenges the automation-first assumption,
then asks for `SEE`. The DM delivers a choice between the 5-minute belief-shift
asset and the 35-minute walkthrough without an email or identity gate. The
content must move the owner from either “that happened this week” or “maybe
automation is not the first question” to “I want to understand how my business
actually works,” then test whether the owner adopts “Operational Reality” as
the name for that object, then “I want to see what StrategicAI would find in
my company,” and finally “I already know what I would ask Nemo.” Attribution
must survive comment/DM transport and both content routes. Offer and checkout
attribution remain dormant instrumentation until the commercial branch is
authorized.

### Relationship to founder-led outbound

Media is credibility and warming infrastructure for:

- warm outreach and referrals;
- cold email and LinkedIn outreach;
- in-person visits and local communities;
- founder follow-up after a first conversation;
- governed follow-up for people who comment `SEE` but do not reach or start content, where platform rules and consent permit it.

Outbound messages should share the clip that matches the prospect’s observed pain rather than a generic company explainer. CRM records should retain both outreach source and clip ID so assisted conversion remains visible.

### Acquisition phases

These phases preserve the commercial plan; they do not authorize the offer.
Until Commercial Commitment closes, their media work is limited to learning,
content, and qualified conversation.

| Phase | Commercial target | Media job | Founder behavior | Production behavior | Decision standard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1: first 10 | Validate paid demand and delivery | Discover which pain states create qualified conversations; provide credible follow-up assets | High-touch outreach, manual follow-up, direct objection capture | Publish all eight; expand only clear signals; do not chase volume | Paid tenants and high-quality applications outrank reach |
| Phase 2: next 90 | Scale proven acquisition paths within 90 days | Increase frequency around proven pain states, retarget warm owners, support referrals | Systematic outreach by pain state, faster follow-up, referral asks | Produce 3–4 angles for winners, reuse proven masters, add paid support only after funnel QA | Cost and capacity-adjusted paid conversion by pain/channel |

### Three complementary creative tracks

The media system has three distinct tracks:

| Track | Code | First reaction | Creative starting point | Primary learning question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pain-state recognition | `PS` | “That happened in my business.” | One concrete owner moment | Which pain states create qualified owner behavior? |
| Category Contrast | `CC` | “Maybe automation is not the first question.” | One familiar automation/agent market moment, including governed direct category attack | Does challenging automation-first thinking create qualified `SEE` and content intent, and which conflict intensity converts qualified owners? |
| Message Card | `MC` | “That sentence names my problem.” | One governed sentence on a controlled branded card | Which exact hook earns qualified `SEE` behavior? |

Both tracks converge first in plain owner language:

```text
I want to understand how my business actually works.
    -> Is “Operational Reality” the clearest name for that?
    -> I want to see what StrategicAI would discover.
    -> I already know what I would ask Nemo.
```

Do not combine the tracks into one analytic leaderboard. A pain-state clip
screens for lived recognition; a Category Contrast clip tests a market belief;
a Message Card tests exact language. Message Cards use
`F100-MC-{TERRITORY}-{NN}` and the manifest defined in the governing `SEE`
contract.

### Canonical category hierarchy

```text
STRATEGICAI = OPERATIONAL_INTELLIGENCE_BUILT_FROM_OPERATIONAL_REALITY
VISIBLE_EXPRESSION = BUSINESS_VIEWS
INTERACTION_LAYER = NEMO
DOWNSTREAM_ACTION = AUTOMATION_AND_CAPABILITIES_WHEN_WARRANTED
PLAIN_ENGLISH_SHORTHAND = AN_AI_THAT_KNOWS_YOUR_BUSINESS
SHORTHAND_ROLE = EXPLANATORY_NOT_PRIMARY_POSITIONING
```

StrategicAI must not be collapsed into “an AI.” The canonical product and
company proposition is Operational Intelligence built from Operational
Reality. Business Views are intended to make bounded parts of that intelligence
visible, Nemo is intended to let the owner reason from lawfully available
context, and automation or other capabilities may act when evidence and
authority warrant them. “An AI that knows your business” may be tested as an
ordinary-language translation, but it is not the primary promise or runtime
proof.

### Primary Category Contrast

```text
PRIMARY_CATEGORY_CONTRAST = AUTOMATION_WITHOUT_UNDERSTANDING
POSITIVE_PRODUCT_TERRITORY = OPERATIONAL_REALITY
TRANSFORMATION = OPERATIONAL_REALITY_TO_OPERATIONAL_INTELLIGENCE
CAMPAIGN_DISTINCTION = UNDERSTANDING_FIRST_NOT_AUTOMATION_FIRST
```

Plain-language definition:

> **StrategicAI starts with how the organization actually works. Automation starts only after the business is understood well enough to know what should happen, why, who is involved, and what else the change will affect.**

The positive sequence is:

```text
Operational Reality
    -> understanding
    -> Operational Intelligence
    -> decision
    -> automation, agents, ownership, process change, notification,
       system connection, human judgment, simplification,
       investigation, or no change
```

The underlying enemy is the starting error: automation without understanding.
TOFU media may name AI automation agencies and automation-first offers as the
category object being criticized. That authorization applies to the premise,
sequence, category behavior, or buying mistake—not unsupported allegations
about specific companies or people. Strong automation specialists may still
use StrategicAI Business Views before implementation or operate downstream as
partners.

### Conflict posture

```text
CONFLICT_IS_A_VALID_ACQUISITION_TOOL = TRUE
CONFLICT_INTENSITY = C0 | C1 | C2 | C3 | C4
```

Category Contrast does not need to sound diplomatically neutral. A hook may
provoke, accuse, contradict, challenge a popular category, attack a common
buying behavior, or create disagreement. Its standard is whether the right
business owner stops, recognizes the issue, reconsiders the dominant
assumption, comments `SEE`, and moves through content toward a truthful next
step. An offer is one possible later step only after commercial authority.

| Level | Posture | Intended owner response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `C0_NEUTRAL` | Educational contrast | “That is sensible.” |
| `C1_CONTRARIAN` | Challenges conventional wisdom | “Maybe the market is starting too late.” |
| `C2_DIRECT_ATTACK` | Names and criticizes the automation-agency category | “Maybe these offers are selling me the wrong thing first.” |
| `C3_HARD_ATTACK` | Accuses the premise or buying behavior | “I may be paying someone to automate a guess.” |
| `C4_MAX_CONFLICT` | Polarizing but truth-governed indictment | “The gold rush may be scaling chaos.” |

Conflict opens attention; Operational Reality must earn the next step. Do not
interpret angry provider comments as owner-market validation. A quiet,
certain attack is preferred to shouting, contempt, or performance rage.

```text

```

### Category Contrast message ladder

```text
Automation/agent market moment
    -> “My feed is full of people trying to automate pieces of my company.”
Conflict
    -> “They may be starting in the wrong place.”
Challenge
    -> “Maybe ‘can AI do this?’ is not the first question.”
Positive territory
    -> “How does the business actually work?”
Transformation
    -> “Operational Reality can become Operational Intelligence.”
Webinar
    -> “Show me what StrategicAI would discover before I automate.”
Founding 100
    -> “Build the understanding, then decide what should change.”
```

### Proposed Founding 100 value hierarchy — commercially gated

The offer should be understood in this order:

1. **StrategicAI is being built to form an evidence-bounded understanding of
   Operational Reality.** Exact company context remains runtime-scoped.
2. **That becomes Operational Intelligence.** The understanding supports better questions and better-scoped intervention choices.
3. **Business Views are intended to make bounded parts of the intelligence
   visible.** The owner may inspect and correct a view present in the accepted
   experience.
4. **Nemo is intended to work from lawfully available context.** Exact context,
   continuity, and behavior require runtime evidence.
5. **A 90-day question experience is proposed.** It is not an active purchase
   promise until runtime, delivery, capacity, support, and commercial gates
   close.
6. **Continuation would be earned through usefulness.** No business outcome is
   guaranteed.

### Business Views: role in the experience

If implemented and commercially included, Business Views may serve as:

- useful deliverables generated from the business discovered;
- ways for the owner to inspect the company;
- proof that StrategicAI is building company understanding;
- correction surfaces that make “No, that is not how it works” productive; and
- inputs that improve the context Nemo reasons from.

They are not a rigid fixed bundle and they are not the hero product by themselves.

### Advisor question bridge

The campaign should create the feeling: **I already know what I would ask Nemo.** Use one real question at a time in clips, short/deep content, outbound follow-up, and discovery:

- “What is still too dependent on me?”
- “Do we really need another person, and what problem would that solve?”
- “Where does this process really break?”
- “What should we fix before we automate?”
- “What would break first if we grew 30%?”
- “Which part of this problem is sales, and which part is operations?”
- “Of these three problems, which deserves attention first?”

These are question examples, not promises that Nemo will answer every question or replace an accountable advisor.

### Advisor framing and consultant comparison

The defensible comparison is about where understanding lives. A human consultant must spend time learning the company before advice becomes specific. StrategicAI is attempting to make company understanding a durable part of the product experience so the owner can return to company-specific context when thinking through a new question.

Use:

- “How long does it normally take a new advisor to really understand your company?”
- “What if that understanding did not have to be rebuilt every time you wanted help thinking through a problem?”
- “Imagine bringing a question to an advisor that already understood the people, systems, bottlenecks, and history behind it.”

Do not use:

- “Nemo knows your company better than any consultant.”
- “Nemo gives better advice than a consultant.”
- “Nemo never forgets.”
- “Nemo replaces your accountant, attorney, consultant, manager, or employees.”

### Campaign anchors

- Canonical category: **Operational Intelligence built from Operational Reality.**
- Plain-English shorthand, not primary positioning: **An AI that knows your business.**
- Experience: **Use Nemo like a flashlight inside your company.**
- Progression: **See it. Challenge it. Change it.**
- Automation reframe: **Understand it before you automate it.**
- Primary category contrast: **Automation without understanding.**
- Positive territory: **Operational Reality—how the business actually works.**
- Strong line to test, not yet canon: **Don’t automate the business you think you have. Understand the business you actually have.**
- Information distinction: **Files are information. They are not understanding.**
- Advisor consequence: **What would you ask if your advisor already knew your business?**
- Simple value line to test, not yet canon: **Build the understanding first. Then bring Nemo the hard questions.**
- Correction principle: the first picture becomes useful because the owner can challenge it.

Use one anchor at a time. Never stack them into slogan soup.

### Main-site narrative implication

Record for the separately authorized main-site rewrite:

```text
Operational Reality
    -> StrategicAI understanding
    -> Operational Intelligence
    -> Business Views
    -> Nemo
    -> capabilities / automation / execution when warranted
```

This is narrative direction, not authority to change the main site in this task.

## B. Audience architecture

### Priority and family map

| Family | Family code | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owner Dependence | `OD` | Human Router; Company-in-My-Head; Firefighter; Can’t-Take-a-Vacation | — | — |
| Visibility | `VIS` | Late Problem; Tool-Stacked but Still Blind | — | Two Versions; What Am I Missing |
| Flow Failure | `FLOW` | Broken Handoff | Customer Chasing Us; Change Doesn’t Travel; Who Owns This | — |
| Capacity + Growth | `CAP` | We Need Another Person | Scaling Chaos; Tribal Knowledge also informs capacity | — |
| Misdiagnosed Solutions | `MIS` | — | Repeated Problem | Automation Hungry; Lost Lead |

Tribal Knowledge belongs primarily to `CAP` for reporting, while its creative interpretation may overlap `OD`. One primary code keeps analysis stable.

### Cross-avatar creative rules

- One clip equals one recognition state and one painful moment.
- Start with the moment, not the avatar label.
- Respect the people inside the problem; the system may be unclear without anyone being incompetent.
- Ask recognition questions instead of diagnosing a viewer’s company.
- Show that the picture can be challenged and corrected.
- Introduce StrategicAI after the reframe, usually after 15 seconds.
- End on `Comment SEE`; the transport delivers the DM choices. Do not add follow, application, and purchase CTAs to the same clip.

### Exclusion filter

Reject a concept before scripting if it:

- uses a demographic caricature instead of a pain moment;
- contains more than one pain family;
- begins with StrategicAI, Nemo, AI, or platform features;
- blames an employee, function, or owner;
- implies a diagnosis without company evidence;
- promises an integration, automatic action, fixed Business View, or business outcome;
- uses generic AI-transformation language;
- cannot be spoken naturally by the founder in one breath at a time.
